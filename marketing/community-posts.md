### Post Content

**Post Title:** The First-Invoice Checklist: The 7 items you must include so you actually get paid on time

```markdown
If you’ve just wrapped up your first freelance client project, congratulations! But before you send over an email asking for money, sending a properly formatted invoice is critical—not just for your records, but so your client's accounting department doesn't delay your payout.

Here is the 7-point checklist every freelance invoice should include before you hit send:

### 1. The Word "INVOICE" & A Unique Invoice Number
- Don't label it "Bill" or "Receipt" (bills are what you receive, receipts are proof of payment after the fact).
- Use a clean numbering scheme: `INV-2026-001` or `INV-001`. Never send an invoice without a reference number, or corporate clients won't be able to match the payment in their accounting ledger.

### 2. Your Full Legal / Business Information
- Your full name (or registered DBA/LLC)
- Email address and phone number
- Physical address (or P.O. Box / city & country if remote)
- Tax ID / VAT / GST / ABN / EIN number if required in your jurisdiction

### 3. Detailed Client Information
- Company name and physical address
- The exact name/department of your point of contact (e.g., "Attn: Jane Doe, Accounts Payable")

### 4. Itemized Scope of Work (Hours vs. Deliverables)
- Avoid vague descriptions like "Design work" or "Consulting".
- Instead, be explicit:
  - *Sprint 1: UI/UX Wireframes & Component Specs (25 hrs @ $75/hr)*
  - or *Fixed Deliverable: 5 Blog Articles (1,200 words each) with SEO meta specs*
- This prevents "scope confusion" when finance verifies the invoice with your project manager.

### 5. Issue Date & Explicit Due Date
- Don't just write "Due upon receipt" unless you already agreed to that.
- Give a calendar date: *"Due Date: March 28, 2026 (Net 14)"*.
- Net 14 (14 days) is typically the sweet spot for solo freelancers dealing with small-to-midsize businesses.

### 6. Multiple, Friction-Free Payment Instructions
- Provide exact routing details: Bank Transfer (ACH / IBAN / Swift), Stripe payment link, Wise, or PayPal.
- Include a note on who covers wire/transfer fees if international.

### 7. Late Payment Terms
- Add one polite line at the bottom: *"A standard late fee of 1.5% per month applies to overdue balances after 30 days."* Even if you waive it, it creates psychological urgency.
```

### Post Content

**Post Title:** Why you should never start freelance client work without a 50% upfront deposit (and the exact wording to use)

```markdown
One of the most painful lessons almost every freelancer learns the hard way is getting "client ghosted" after submitting work. 

You spend 30 hours building a website or designing brand guidelines, send the final deliverables, and suddenly emails bounce, replies take 3 weeks, or the client claims "budget priorities shifted."

Here is why a 50% upfront deposit is mandatory for any project over $500, and how to position it professionally without sounding insecure:

### Why 50% Upfront is Standard Industry Practice
1. **Skin in the game:** A client who won't pay a deposit rarely pays the final invoice on time. The deposit acts as a quick litmus test for client creditworthiness.
2. **Covers initial hard costs & time:** If the client disappears 2 weeks in, your rent and baseline time are still protected.
3. **Establishes parity:** You are an independent business partner, not an unpaid speculative contractor.

### The Exact Milestone Wording for Your Contract & Invoice

**On your contract / proposal:**
> "Project kickoff is scheduled upon receipt of the initial 50% commencement deposit. The remaining 50% balance is invoiced upon final milestone delivery, prior to transfer of production credentials and source files."

**On your first invoice:**
- **Item 1:** *50% Commencement Deposit — Website Redesign & CMS Migration ($1,500.00)*
- **Terms:** *Due immediately prior to scheduled kickoff date.*
- **Notes:** *Remaining 50% balance ($1,500.00) due upon final preview approval before domain launch.*

### How to Handle Clients Who Say "Our Policy is Net 30 Post-Completion"
If an enterprise client pushes back, offer milestone tranches:
- 33% upfront commencement
- 33% at intermediate milestone (wireframes / staging environment)
- 34% at final delivery

Never do 100% upon completion unless you have an existing multi-year relationship with high trust.
```

## 📝 Post 3: Net 30 vs. Due on Receipt — The Payment Term Trap
**Best Venues:**
- `r/freelance` (Weekly Promotion thread)
- `r/webdev` Showcase thread
- Indie Hackers Solopreneurs
- Personal LinkedIn / X thread

### Post Content

**Post Title:** Net 30 vs. Due on Receipt: The payment term trap that keeps new freelancers unpaid for 60+ days

```markdown
When you issue an invoice to a client, the payment term you choose often dictates whether you get paid in 5 days or 65 days.

Here is a quick breakdown of what these terms actually mean in practice, and how new freelancers can avoid getting trapped in corporate cash flow limbo:

### 1. "Due on Receipt"
- **What it means:** The client should technically pay as soon as they receive the invoice.
- **The reality:** Consumers understand this, but corporate accounts payable departments often view it as ambiguous. If you don't give a specific calendar date, AP puts it at the back of their weekly or bi-weekly check run.
- **When to use:** Short fixed gigs, initial project deposits, and retail/trade services.

### 2. "Net 30" (The Trap)
- **What it means:** Payment is due 30 calendar days from the invoice date.
- **The trap:** In big companies, Net 30 often turns into **Net 45 or Net 60**. 
  - Why? You send the invoice on March 1.
  - The project manager takes 10 days to approve it.
  - Accounting enters it on March 12.
  - The 30 days starts *from entry*, not invoice date.
  - The payment run is only on the 1st and 15th of the month.
  - Result: You don't receive funds until May 1st!

### 3. The Sweet Spot: "Net 14" with a Specific Calendar Date
For solo freelancers, designers, and software contractors, **Net 14** is the gold standard:
- It gives clients two full business weeks to process through accounting.
- It prevents you from waiting an entire month to find out if there's an issue with their payment method.
- **Always print the exact deadline date:** Don't just write "Net 14"; write:
  *Payment Due: April 14, 2026 (Net 14)*.

### Pro-Tip: Add Late Fee Terms to Every Invoice
Even a polite 1.5% late fee term drastically improves payment speed:
> "Payment terms: Net 14. Accounts past 30 days are subject to a 1.5% monthly late finance charge."
```
