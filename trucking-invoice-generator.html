Freight Invoicing Fundamentals for Commercial Motor Carriers

In freight transportation, timely payment relies on having a water-tight paperwork trail. Brokers and corporate shippers will delay processing if key accessorial items or documentation references are absent.

1. Motor Carrier Authority (USDOT & MC #)

Federal regulations and broker compliance departments require proof of your active operating authority. Add your USDOT # and MC # directly beneath your company name.

2. Load Reference & Rate Confirmation Number

Every freight invoice must reference the broker’s Load / Rate Con ID and the Bill of Lading (BOL) number. Shippers use these reference codes to reconcile your invoice against their receiving warehouse logs.

3. Linehaul vs. Accessorial Charges

Do not lump accessorial charges into the primary freight rate. Itemize:

  • Linehaul Freight: Agreed base rate or per-mile charge.
  • Fuel Surcharge (FSC): Adjusted according to the Department of Energy weekly diesel index.
  • Detention: Billed hourly (standard is after 2 hours of free time at shipper/receiver).
  • Lumper / Pallet Fees: Pass-through unloading charges with receipt attached.
  • Layover / TONU: Truck Ordered Not Used fees if the broker cancelled at the dock.

4. QuickPay vs. Net 30 Terms

Specify whether you offer a standard Net 30 turnaround or a 2% QuickPay discount for payment disbursed within 48 to 72 hours.
