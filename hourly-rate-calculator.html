The Freelance Pricing Formula: Why Most Freelancers Undercharge

The single biggest mistake new independent professionals make is taking their previous full-time corporate salary and dividing it by 2,080 hours (40 hours × 52 weeks). For example, a $80,000 employee might conclude they should charge $38.50 per hour. In reality, charging $38.50 as a 1099 contractor will leave you working 60-hour weeks while earning less than minimum wage after taxes and expenses.

The 3 Hidden Cost Factors Every Contractor Must Cover:

  1. Non-Billable Time: You cannot bill 40 hours every week. Between prospecting clients, writing proposals, sending invoices, bookkeeping, and client communications, full-time freelancers average only 20 to 28 billable hours per week.
  2. Self-Employment Taxes & Benefits: Traditional W-2 employers contribute 7.65% for FICA/Medicare, subsidize group health insurance, and match retirement contributions. As a freelancer, you must pay both the employer and employee portions of self-employment tax.
  3. Business Overhead: High-end software subscriptions (Adobe Creative Cloud, Figma, GitHub, Google Workspace), accounting fees, home office utilities, and equipment depreciation must be paid out of gross revenues.

The Exact Mathematical Formula Used by This Tool

📐 The Sustainable Rate Formula:
Gross Revenue Needed = (Target Net Salary + Overhead Expenses) / (1 - Tax Rate Percentage)
Annual Billable Hours = (52 Weeks - Vacation Weeks) × Weekly Billable Hours
Hourly Rate = Gross Revenue Needed / Annual Billable Hours

Hourly Rates vs. Project-Based & Value Pricing

While an hourly rate provides a safe baseline to prevent working at a loss, seasoned consultants often package services into fixed milestone deliverables or value-based tiers:
