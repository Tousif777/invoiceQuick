How to Stop Undercharging for Handyman Services

Many independent handymen operate at razor-thin margins because they fail to account for fuel, drive time, consumables (screws, caulk, tape), and tool wear. Setting your pricing structure right is the difference between thriving and burning out.

Hourly Rates vs. Flat-Rate Pricing

Pricing Model When to Use Pros & Cons
Hourly ($65–$120/hr) Mystery leaks, rot repair, multi-item honey-do lists Guarantees pay for surprises; client may monitor clock
Flat-Rate (Per Task) Ceiling fan install ($150), garbage disposal ($175), door hang ($220) Higher profit margins for fast workers; risk of scope creep

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average hourly rate for a handyman in 2026?

Handymen typically charge between $60 and $125 per hour depending on geographic location, licensing, and job complexity.

Should a handyman charge a service call or trip fee?

Yes. Most professional handymen charge a minimum 1 to 2-hour minimum trip fee ($75–$150) to cover transit fuel and tool setup.

How much should a handyman mark up materials?

A standard material markup is 15% to 30% to compensate for shopping time, fuel, transit wear, and warranty liability.
