Navigating Subcontractor Billing Cycles

Subcontractors face unique billing hurdles. Unlike simple freelance gigs, trade billing involves complex general contractor (GC) approval chains, architect sign-offs, lien waivers, and retainage deductions.

Whether you work in carpentry, dry-walling, HVAC, or electrical, use our Contractor Invoice Generator or printable Blank Invoice PDF forms to get paid on time.

Essential Fields on a Professional Subcontractor Invoice

  1. Project & Job Site Reference: State the exact property address, lot number, or commercial building suite.
  2. General Contractor Contact & PO #: Include the GC's project manager name and approved Subcontract Agreement ID.
  3. Phase or Milestone Breakdown: Clearly state which schedule of values phase is being billed (e.g. Phase 2: Rough-In Plumbing).
  4. Separation of Materials & Labor: Itemize bulk supplies with receipts attached and billable journeyman labor hours.
  5. Retainage Calculation: Clearly list the gross amount, subtract the 10% retainage holdback, and display the net balance payable today.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is retainage on a construction subcontractor invoice?

Retainage is a percentage (typically 5% to 10%) withheld by the general contractor until the entire project is completed satisfactorily.

Do subcontractors need a purchase order (PO) number to invoice?

Yes, most general contractors will reject invoices that do not reference the master contract number or approved Purchase Order (PO).

Should subcontractors separate labor and materials on invoices?

Yes. Separating materials from labor prevents sales tax audit disputes and provides clear cost justification for progress draws.
