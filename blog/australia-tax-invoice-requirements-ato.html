Australian Taxation Office (ATO) Compliance

In Australia, issuing a compliant bill is essential so your clients can claim Goods and Services Tax (GST) credits on their Business Activity Statements (BAS). The Australian Taxation Office (ATO) maintains strict guidelines on what constitutes a valid Tax Invoice.

The 7 Mandatory ATO Requirements for Sales Under A$1,000

  1. Document Heading: Must clearly state "Tax Invoice" at the top.
  2. Seller Identity: Your legal entity name or registered business trading name.
  3. Seller's ABN: Your 11-digit Australian Business Number.
  4. Date of Issue: Date the invoice is created.
  5. Brief Description: Clear itemization of services or goods supplied.
  6. GST Amount: Clearly states the GST amount (10%) or states that the "Total price includes GST".
  7. Extent of Tax: Clearly shows which items are taxable and which are GST-free.

Additional Requirement for Sales of A$1,000 or More

When the total invoice balance equals or exceeds A$1,000 (including GST), the ATO requires one additional piece of information:

The buyer's identity or the buyer's ABN must be explicitly printed in the "Bill To" section of the document. Without the buyer's details, the ATO will deny your client's input tax credit claims!

Frequently Asked Questions

When is an Australian business required to register for GST?

You must register for GST with the ATO if your annual business turnover is A$75,000 or more (or A$150,000 for non-profit entities).

What is the difference between an invoice and an ATO Tax Invoice?

Only businesses registered for GST can issue a document titled 'Tax Invoice'. Non-registered businesses issue standard invoices without GST.

What is the ATO $1,000 tax invoice rule?

For sales of A$1,000 or more, the tax invoice must additionally display the buyer's identity or ABN to allow the buyer to claim GST credits.
