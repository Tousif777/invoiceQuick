Why Importers & Exporters Must Know the Difference

In global trade and logistics, issuing the wrong documentation can lead to severe border delays, customs penalties, or buyers having their shipments held in bonded warehouses. The two most common documents in export shipping are the Proforma Invoice and the Commercial Invoice.

What is a Proforma Invoice?

A proforma invoice is an initial or estimated bill sent by a seller to a buyer before any products are manufactured or dispatched. It serves as an official offer to sell, outlining the exact items, estimated freight costs, currency, and payment terms.

Buyers typically request a proforma invoice to:

  • Apply for an import permit or import license from their domestic commerce ministry.
  • Open a Letter of Credit (LC) with their commercial bank.
  • Arrange foreign exchange wire transfers before goods embark.

What is a Commercial Invoice?

A commercial invoice is the definitive legal accounting record issued once an international shipment is packaged and ready for dispatch. It acts as both the formal demand for payment and the primary evidentiary document utilized by customs authorities across the globe to compute import duties, Value Added Tax (VAT), and tariffs.

Every valid commercial invoice must feature:

  1. Full Shipper & Consignee Details: Legal business names, tax IDs (EORI number in the EU, EIN in the US), addresses, and contact numbers.
  2. Itemized Product Classification: Detailed goods descriptions paired with international 6 to 10-digit Harmonized System (HS) Codes.
  3. Country of Origin: The physical manufacturing origin of each commodity.
  4. Agreed Incoterms 2020: Clear definition of liability (e.g. FOB Shanghai, EXW Munich, CIF Los Angeles).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a proforma invoice legally binding?

No, a proforma invoice is a preliminary quotation sent to help the buyer arrange import licenses or bank financing. Only the commercial invoice acts as a legally binding bill of sale.

Can customs clear goods using a proforma invoice?

In most jurisdictions, customs requires a final Commercial Invoice to assess tariffs and duties. A proforma invoice is only accepted if goods are samples or no money is exchanged.

Does a commercial invoice require HS codes?

Yes. Accurate 6 to 10-digit Harmonized System (HS) tariff codes must be included for each line item on a commercial invoice to prevent border holds.
