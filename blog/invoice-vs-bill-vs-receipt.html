Invoice vs Bill vs Receipt: Differences & Examples

MT
MD Tousif
Updated Nov 17, 2026 • 5 min read

An invoice requests payment. A bill also describes money owed, often from the customer's perspective. A receipt confirms an amount actually paid. The same payment request may be an invoice to the seller and a bill to the buyer; the label alone does not determine whether it has been paid.

Invoice, bill and receipt at a glance
DocumentPurposeWho usually supplies it?Does it confirm payment?
InvoiceItemizes a charge and asks for payment under agreed terms.Seller or service providerNo, unless it also records a verified payment.
BillShows an amount owed, such as a restaurant or utility charge.Seller or service provider; the buyer records it as payable.No.
ReceiptAcknowledges a payment and identifies what it relates to.Recipient of the paymentYes, for the amount shown; it may be a partial payment.

Invoice vs bill: often the same transaction

A designer sends a client an invoice for a project. The client may call that document a bill and record it as money they owe. A restaurant also issues a bill before taking payment. Customers do not generally create the supplier's bill themselves.

Use invoice when you need itemized services, a reference number and agreed payment terms. Use bill if that is the familiar wording for your customer. What matters is that the amount, supplier, customer, dates and payment instructions are clear.

Invoice vs receipt: a request and an acknowledgement

An invoice can be issued for a deposit, a project milestone or completed work, according to the agreement. It can later be unpaid, partly paid or paid. A receipt acknowledges money received, including a deposit; it does not have to wait until the whole invoice is settled.

An unpaid invoice is not proof that money changed hands. If you record payment on an invoice, show the payment date, amount received and remaining balance. A PAID label should reflect a confirmed payment.

Example: a 500 invoice and a 200 deposit

These illustrative amounts use one currency and exclude tax:

  1. Request: the provider issues invoice INV-104 for 500. The client records a bill for 500.
  2. Deposit received: the client pays 200. The provider issues a receipt for 200 referencing INV-104.
  3. Balance: the invoice now shows total 500, amount paid 200 and balance due 300.
  4. Final payment: after receiving the remaining 300, the provider records it and provides a receipt or payment acknowledgement. The balance is zero.

Common questions

Is a bill a receipt?

No. A bill shows a charge or an amount owed. A receipt acknowledges payment. One document can serve both functions if it clearly records the charge and the payment received.

Can an invoice be issued before work starts?

Yes, for example to request an agreed deposit. State what is being billed and the agreed terms. Do not imply that work has been completed if it has not.

Does a receipt always mean paid in full?

No. A receipt can acknowledge a partial payment. Include the amount received and any remaining balance so the customer is not misled.

Does changing the heading make a document compliant?

No. The heading does not validate the contents or record an actual bank transaction. Required fields depend on the transaction and applicable rules.
