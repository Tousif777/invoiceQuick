What Every Plumbing Contractor Invoice Must Include

Plumbing jobs range from minor faucet leaks to full underground sewer line replacements. A detailed, professional invoice protects your business from payment disputes and establishes credibility with residential and commercial clients.

1. Plumber License & Certification Information

State licensing boards and insurance carriers require plumbers to display their Master or Journeyman Plumbing License number (e.g., "Lic #PL-89421"). Adding this to your invoice header reassures clients of code-compliant craftsmanship.

2. Diagnostic / Service Call Fee

If you charge a standard trip fee or diagnostic charge, list it as a dedicated line item. If the fee is waived upon accepting the repair quote, specify the discount clearly.

3. Separation of Physical Fixtures vs. Skilled Labor

List physical supplies (e.g., PEX tubing, shut-off valves, wax rings, water heater units) separate from your labor hours or flat job rates. Homeowners appreciate seeing the exact parts installed in their property.

4. Code Compliance & Workmanship Warranty

State your warranty terms in the notes section (e.g., "All pipe joints and fittings covered by a 12-month parts and labor guarantee. Work completed to 2026 Uniform Plumbing Code.").
